01
Purpose and Principle
#

Kairos Systems publishes a verifier, the hashes this verifier produces, and the declared scope of validity of that claim. This protocol allows a third party to recompute them on its own environment, without any intervention by Kairos Systems.

The objective is not to ask the validator to trust Kairos Systems. The objective is to enable the validator to reproduce the published results independently. Any observed discrepancy takes precedence over Kairos Systems' claims.

This document is self-contained. It requires no assistance from Kairos Systems to be executed.

What Is Requested

Execute the protocol on an environment controlled by the validator.

Record the hashes obtained.

Produce a factual attestation of what was observed.

What Is Not Requested

No validation, no certification, no audit.

No judgment on the Kairos product.

No conclusion beyond what was personally executed.

Characterization of This Exercise

This exercise constitutes an external replay.

It constitutes neither an independent validation, nor a certification, nor a regulatory audit.

Kairos Systems commits to using none of these terms in reference to this exercise.

Guided Verification Tool
#

The tool executes both parts of the protocol, compares the hashes obtained against the published hashes, and records the whole in an attestation data file.

This tool measures and records. It does not interpret. A measurement discrepancy is recorded as observed and execution continues; the tool never characterizes a result as acceptable or unacceptable.

Only a technical impossibility — an inaccessible repository, an environment that cannot be created — interrupts execution, and the document then explicitly flags this as not being a verification result.

Reading the Discrepancies

The L3 matrix product takes four distinct values depending on the BLAS implementation actually loaded: OpenBLAS on Linux, Apple's Accelerate library on macOS, in three variants depending on architecture and chip generation (Intel x86_64, Apple M1, Apple M2). The two L6 measurements, delegated to the xgboost library, take a value specific to each platform, identical between single-thread and multi-thread mode. On macOS, outside the declared envelope, the L3 level (BLAS matmul) shows different values depending on the physical environment tested: Intel x86_64, Apple M1, and Apple M2. The virtualized M2 Pro runner reproduces the value obtained on M1. This observation shows a dependency of the L3 result on the execution environment; it does not, on its own, allow causal attribution of this variation to chip generation or virtualization. The L5 and L6 levels show, across the tested Apple Silicon environments, the same divergences from the canonical reference.

On Apple Silicon architecture, the L5 aggregation also produces a distinct value.

These discrepancies characterize the compute substrate, not the measurement instrument:

each platform produces a value that is stable and reproducible on itself.

They do not affect the reconstruction of the artifact, which arrives at the published hash on all three platforms.

01Context#

A data-processing pipeline that does not replay identically cannot be audited.

In a regulated environment, the requirement is not that the system works — it is that one can demonstrate, months later, that what was executed is exactly what is declared.

Most systems claim this property. Few make it verifiable by a third party.

Kairos Systems publishes its verifier, its expected hashes, and its declared scope of validity.

This demonstration does not ask for trust in Kairos — it asks for verification.

05Declared Scope of Validity#

The results claimed in this demonstration are limited to the execution conditions explicitly described below.

Two distinct envelopes apply, to two different objects.

Acts 1 and 2 do not measure the same thing and do not carry the same dependencies.

Envelope A — digital substrate, Act 1
numpy==2.2.6  pandas==2.3.3  xgboost==3.2.0
OPENBLAS_CORETYPE=Haswell
OMP_NUM_THREADS=1  MKL_NUM_THREADS=1  OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1
PYTHONHASHSEED=0
Envelope B — artifact reconstruction, Act 2
pandas==1.5.3  pyarrow==23.0.0  numpy<2

The two differ for a measured reason.

The Parquet format embeds the versions of the writing libraries in the bytes of the resulting file.

The artifact recorded on 2026-06-06 carries created_by:

parquet-cpp-arrow version 23.0.0 and pandas_version: 1.5.3.

Reconstructing it identically therefore requires these exact versions.

Every element of these envelopes is a declared, controlled variable.
